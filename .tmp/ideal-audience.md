# The perfect average reader: Alex

**Age:** 28

**Education:** A university degree in a non-technical field—perhaps communications or geography. Alex studied science at school and remembers the basics, but hasn’t worked with equations since.

**Work:** Alex works in policy or project management, in a role adjacent to technology but not directly involved in building it. They’re used to reading about unfamiliar subjects and asking, “Why does this matter?”

**Interests and habits:** They follow space news, watch the occasional science YouTube video, and might have heard of SAR or the James Webb Space Telescope without knowing how either works. They’ve clicked through because “we can’t download all the data from space” sounds surprising.

**What they need from the article:** A clear thread connecting the examples back to the data bottleneck. They’re willing to learn a technical term when it helps explain something interesting, but don’t want to stop and research every paragraph.

**What they bring:** Curiosity, patience for an informal tangent, and enough background to appreciate the jokes—but not enough specialist knowledge to fill in an unexplained jump.

Alex isn’t every reader; they’re a useful person to have in mind when deciding what needs explanation and what can be left alone.
