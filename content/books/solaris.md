I am once again undecided whether this gets a 🟩 or a ⭐, so I will leave them both up, and reserve the right to change my opinion later. This was reccomended to me by [HamaonOverdrive](https://hamaonoverdrive.online/) after she had a look at all the other SciFi on my library page. And it was a fantastic reccomendation. It was another book which had me going to go and stare at a wall for an hour or two after finishing to digest its ideas. Also there are apparently two different translations of the book, and the "good" one is out of print. So I read the frankentranslation which went from Polish to French to English. I found it quite enjoyable, but I do wonder what I missed out on. 

Starting with the good, the book deals with really weird freaking aliens in a way I really enjoy. It is clear that there is *something* going on with the goop. The book well and truely pre-dates [Assembly Theory](https://caffeineandlasers.com/books/life-as-no-one-knows-it.html) but I am sure that Sara Imari Walker would describe it as alive. In anycase the book really wrangles with how strange conciousness, intelligence and life really is. Thankfully the book never gives into the temptation to explain itself. The alien is just as alien to us at the end of the story and at the start. (If it even is an alien). 

I do have my criticisms of the book. Once you get a few chapters in, the book spends about half of its time lore dumping, with the narrator relaying what he has read in the books in the station library. I echo [Rebecca Watson's](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=feMoj88mu-U) crtique that these sections kill the momentum. On the other hand the scenes when the protagonist is interacting with the crewmates can carry so much tension at times, it is fantastic. 

In many ways it the story reminded me of Bloodborne. The way the  <span class="spoiler" tabindex="0"> survivors lock themselves in and hardly interact with eachother,</span>, the unknowable secrets of the universe, and the <span class="spoiler" tabindex="0">Defective God hypothesis. </span> It all evokes the same discomfort and unknowableness of the eldritch alien gods of Bloodborne. 

While reviewing my thoughts on the book I stumbled accross [this youtube video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=niIZVU-0fZg&pp=ygUHU29sYXJpcw%3D%3D) which has massive spoilers, but I think really encaspulates the main points and themes of the book quite well.
