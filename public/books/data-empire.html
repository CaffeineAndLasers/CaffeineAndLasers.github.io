Data Empire: The Power of Information to Organize, Control, and Dominate

by Roopika Risam

Rating: 🟥

Type: Non-Fiction

Read: October 2026

My Thoughts

Sigh Sorry Roopika. The concept for this book was great, but its execution and prose let it down. Especially the prose.

The thesis of Risam's book, is that throughout history the collection of data has been a critical tool in the establishment and reinforcement of political power. This argument, extends all the way from pre-history where debts are tracked by different culutres with various trinkets, all the way to the modern surveilance-capitalist system. It is a decent argument, and one I agree with in principal.

Unfortunately, the much of the prose was clearly written by an LLM. Or at least it felt that it was. I have no proof. This makes for a tiring reading experience as everything is laced with the frenetic ChatGPT-4o stoccatto sentence structure and eye-rolling metaphors.

I was also not totally sold on the argument for her thesis. While I generally think that she is right, the book acts more like a series of observations of cases where data is used to consolidate power, rather than a mechanistic explaantion of exactly why this is the case.

For example, Noam Chomsky's "Manafacturing Consent" spends two or three chapters outlining the exact mechanism he is describing before he leans into any case study, which is why it makes such a compelling argument. The grand arc of history narrative the Risam uses here tells a nice story, but leads to a weak argument in my opinion.

I wanted to like this book, but by the end I had to force myself to finish it which was a shame.
